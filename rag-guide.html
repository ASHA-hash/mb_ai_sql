📖 Part 1 — The Big Picture

What is RAG — in plain English?

RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Forget the jargon. Here is what it actually means for you:

❌ Without RAG

The AI is writing a blind exam

You ask: "What were my top 5 branches by sales last month?"

The AI has never seen your data. It doesn't know that your sales column is called SaleNetAmount, or that "Branch" in your system uses BranchAlias. It guesses — and often gets it wrong.

Result: wrong column names, SQL errors, or worse — silently incorrect numbers.

✅ With RAG

The AI has your company's textbook open

Before answering, the AI automatically looks up your database structure, your business definitions, and your past successful queries.

It finds: SaleNetAmount, BranchAlias, SaleDate in VwAISalesData — and knows "MTD = 1st of month to today".

Result: accurate SQL, correct column names, right business logic. Every time.

✈️

The Travel Agent Analogy

Imagine you walk into a travel agency and ask "Book me the best trip to Manali in December."

❌ New agent (no RAG)

Googles from scratch, doesn't know your budget, doesn't know you prefer trains over flights, doesn't know you've already been to Shimla and want something new. Makes a generic recommendation.

✅ Your agent (with RAG)

Pulls up your folder: budget ₹20K, prefers trains, last trip was Shimla. Instantly recommends Kasol by Chandigarh train — tailored, accurate, no generic nonsense.

RAG is your AI's personal folder about your business. It makes every answer specific to you.

👨‍💼

The New Employee Analogy

Think about hiring two analysts for your finance team:

Day 1 Hire (no RAG)

Smart person, but knows nothing about your company. Ask "What's our MTD Net Sales for North Zone?" — they'll ask: "What's MTD? Which column? Which zone codes?" Needs a week to learn your system.

Has your entire company manual. Knows MTD means SaleDate >= 1st of month, knows Net Sales is SaleNetAmount, knows North Zone branches. Answers instantly and correctly.

RAG is the company manual that turns a generic AI into your trained business analyst.

The 🧠 RAG Memory panel in your dashboard has three distinct stores. Think of them as three shelves in your AI's library:

🗄

Schema Chunks

Your database structure — 28 views with every column name, data type, and description. The AI uses this to know exactly which table and column to query.

⚡ Auto-indexed — no action needed

➕ How to Add a Glossary Term

Use this whenever the AI doesn't understand your internal business language. For example — what does "LY", "PY", "Zone A", or "Net Collection" mean specifically in your context?

1
Go to 🧠 RAG Memory in the left navigation Click the "🧠 RAG Memory" item in the left sidebar of your dashboard.
2
Click the 📖 Glossary tab Inside RAG Memory, select the Glossary tab to see existing terms and the add form.
3
Use the quick-add chips OR type your own For common terms: click a chip (e.g. MTD) to auto-fill the form. For custom terms, type the Term name and its meaning in your database (e.g. Term: LY Sales — Definition: Last Year same period — use SaleNetAmount with SaleDate offset by 1 year).
4
Click "Add Term" — done immediately No restart needed. The next AI Query you run will already use this definition.
💡

Business-language tip: Be specific in your definitions. Instead of "LY = last year", write "LY = Last Year — same SaleDate period shifted back 12 months using SaleNetAmount column in VwAISalesData". The more specific, the more accurate the AI.

📚 How to Add a Query Example Manually

Examples are the most powerful thing you can add. When the AI sees a question similar to one in its memory, it uses the stored SQL as a guide and gets it right almost every time.

A
Automatic (nothing to do) Every successful query runs → auto-saved to Examples. The system learns as you work. Over time, the AI builds up a library of your most common queries.
B
Manual: go to 📚 Examples tab, click "Add Example" Type the question as you'd naturally ask it, paste the correct SQL, and optionally add a note (e.g. "Always use SaleNetAmount, not SaleGrossAmount for this report").
C

❓ Part 7 — Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

🔧 Do I need to set RAG up or configure anything?
No. The schema has already been indexed (28 database views with all columns). RAG Memory is active and running the moment you start using AI Query. The only thing you should do is add Glossary terms — particularly the 5 core ones: MTD, YTD, QTD, Net Sales, and Branch. That takes about 2 minutes and makes an immediate, noticeable difference.
📚 How many examples do I need to add before it gets noticeably better?
You don't need to add any manually — the system auto-saves from your AI Query usage. After about 20 queries, the AI starts recognising your most common patterns. After 50–100 queries, it's typically very accurate. If you use it regularly, quality improves continuously without you doing anything extra.
🔒 Does RAG store my actual sales or customer data?
No. RAG only stores three things: (1) database structure (column names and view descriptions — not data rows), (2) business term definitions you write, and (3) question → SQL pairs. It never stores actual sales figures, customer names, transaction records, or any personal data. It's a map, not the territory.
❌ What if the AI keeps giving the wrong answer for a specific question?
Write the correct SQL (or have your IT team provide it), then go to 🧠 RAG Memory → 📚 Examples → Add Example. Paste the question and the correct SQL. From that point on, whenever the AI sees a similar question, it will use your correct version as a guide — overriding whatever it was generating before.
🗑 Can I delete a wrong example or an outdated glossary term?
Yes. Each entry in both the Examples and Glossary tabs has a delete (✕) button. Removing an example is immediate — the AI will no longer use it as a reference. This is useful when your database structure changes or a business definition is updated.
📋 What's the difference between RAG Memory and SQL Templates?
They serve different purposes. RAG Memory works silently in the background — it makes the AI smarter by providing context for every AI Query. You never "click" it directly. SQL Templates (in 📋 Saved SQL Queries) are named shortcuts you save and click manually to run a specific SQL instantly — bypassing AI generation entirely. Think of RAG as the AI's brain, and SQL Templates as your personal shortcut buttons.
🌐 If I add a glossary term, does it apply to all users?
Yes. Glossary terms, query examples, and SQL templates are stored on the server and shared across all users of the dashboard. If your finance team adds a definition for "Net Collection", all users — including the ops team and the director — will benefit from it immediately.
📈 Will it ever stop getting better?
RAG improves as long as you continue using AI Query and occasionally curate the examples. Think of it like Google Maps — the more people use it, the more accurate it gets. There's no ceiling. However, if your database structure changes significantly (new views, renamed columns), you may need to refresh the schema index — contact your system administrator for that.
